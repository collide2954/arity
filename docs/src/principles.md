# Principles

## arity reports, and it does not judge

arity records what a source states. It does not average values, weigh
sources, or select a best value. When two sources disagree, arity shows two
reports. When one article states two values for the same measurement, for
example one in the abstract and one in the results, arity keeps both.

A statement of no effect is data. "No effect of A on B" becomes an effect with
the direction `no_change`, and the tables show it as 0.

## Each report has its evidence

Each report has quotes from its source. A quote is an exact part of one block
of the canonical text: a paragraph, a table cell, or a caption. The checks find
each quote in its block, and the website marks each quote in the PDF.

## Each record has its provenance

arity records the source files with their addresses and digests, the canonical
text with its digest, the reader and its version, the responsible person, each
check with its person and its time, and the software that did each step. See
[Provenance](provenance.md).

## Notices do not remove data

A retraction, a correction, or an expression of concern does not remove the
reports of a work. arity shows each notice as its source states it, next to the
data. See [Notices](notices.md).

## Open access only

arity reads only works that anyone can read without payment. The website shows
the full text and the PDF of a work only when its license lets others share
them. See [Licenses](licenses.md).

## People check the reports

A report is unchecked until a person compares it with its source. The website
shows unchecked reports with a label. A person can confirm or reject a report,
and arity records who did it and when. A rejected report stays in the
database. The figures hide or dim rejected reports, and you can show them.
